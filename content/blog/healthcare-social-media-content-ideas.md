Consistency is the hardest part of social media for busy practices. The other challenge in healthcare is staying within ethical advertising rules — no testimonials, no guaranteed results, no fear-mongering.

Here are 40 ideas that are useful for patients, good for your brand and comfortable within HPCSA guidelines.

## Educational posts (patients' questions answered)

1. Myth vs fact about a common condition in your field.
2. "When should you see a doctor about…?" guidance.
3. How to prepare for a first appointment.
4. What happens during a routine procedure, step by step.
5. The difference between two commonly confused conditions.
6. Seasonal advice — flu season, summer sun safety, back-to-school checks.
7. A simple home-care tip (stretch, brushing technique, posture).
8. How to read a prescription label or medicine leaflet.
9. Questions to ask your practitioner at your next visit.
10. Warning signs that need urgent care (and the numbers to call).

## Health awareness days

11. World Health Day (7 April).
12. Mental health awareness — October is Mental Health Awareness Month in South Africa.
13. Breast cancer awareness (October) and Movember (November).
14. World Diabetes Day (14 November).
15. Oral Health Month (September in South Africa).
16. World AIDS Day (1 December).
17. Heart Awareness Month (September).
18. Pharmacy Week or Nursing Week for those professions.

## Behind the scenes and people

19. Meet the team — one team member per post.
20. A day in the life at the practice.
21. Why the practitioner chose their profession.
22. Sterilisation and hygiene processes (reassuring for anxious patients).
23. New equipment and what it means for patients.
24. Staff training and continuing professional development.
25. Celebrating team birthdays or practice anniversaries.

## Practical practice information

26. Opening hours, especially around public holidays.
27. Which medical aids you're contracted with.
28. Parking and directions — a short video walk from the car park.
29. How to book via WhatsApp or online.
30. Your cancellation policy, explained kindly.
31. New services or practitioners joining.

## Community and engagement

32. A local charity, school or sports club you support.
33. A health talk or screening day you're hosting.
34. "Ask us anything" — invite general questions (answer in general terms, never individual advice).
35. Polls on health habits ("How many glasses of water did you drink today?").
36. Share a useful resource from a reputable health organisation.

## Formats that perform well

37. Short reels explaining one concept in under 30 seconds.
38. Carousels: "5 things to know about…".
39. Simple infographics with one clear message.
40. Quote cards from the practitioner on a health principle.

## Compliance checklist before you post

- Is everything factual and not exaggerated?
- No patient testimonials or patient-identifiable images without explicit written consent?
- No promises of outcomes?
- No comparisons with other practitioners?
- No individual medical advice in comments?

Our [HPCSA advertising rules guide](/blog/hpcsa-advertising-rules-marketing-guide/) covers this in more depth.

## How often should a practice post?

Two to three quality posts a week is a realistic, effective goal for most practices. Consistency matters more than volume — a planned monthly calendar makes it manageable.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which platforms are best for medical practices?**
Facebook and Instagram reach most South African patients; TikTok can work well for practices comfortable on video; LinkedIn suits specialists who rely on referrals.

**Can we reply to medical questions in comments?**
Give general information only and invite the person to book a consultation. Never diagnose or advise individuals publicly.

**Should practitioners appear on camera?**
It helps a lot. Patients want to know who they'll see. Short, friendly videos build trust quickly.
