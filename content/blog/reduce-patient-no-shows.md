Every empty appointment slot is lost income you can't recover — and a patient who may not have received the care they needed. The good news is that most no-shows are not deliberate. Patients forget, their plans change, or rescheduling feels like too much effort.

These nine strategies make it easy for patients to keep — or properly cancel — their appointments.

## 1. Send reminders on the channel patients actually read

SMS and email work, but in South Africa WhatsApp messages are often read fastest. A good rhythm for most practices:

- **Booking confirmation** immediately.
- **Reminder** 48 hours before.
- **Final reminder** on the morning of, or the day before for early appointments.

Keep reminders short: date, time, practitioner, address, and how to reschedule.

## 2. Ask patients to confirm

A reminder that asks for a reply ("Reply YES to confirm or RESCHEDULE to change your time") turns a passive message into a commitment — and gives you early warning of gaps you can fill.

## 3. Make rescheduling easy

If the only way to cancel is calling during office hours, many patients simply don't show up. Allow rescheduling via WhatsApp, a link or an online form.

## 4. Publish a clear, fair cancellation policy

Explain your policy on your website, booking confirmation and at reception — for example, how much notice you need and whether a fee applies for missed appointments. Apply it consistently and with compassion for genuine emergencies.

## 5. Keep a waitlist

Maintain a short list of patients who'd like an earlier appointment. When someone cancels, message the waitlist. Confirmed cancellations become filled slots instead of lost time.

## 6. Reduce the gap between booking and appointment

The longer the wait, the higher the chance of a no-show. Where possible, keep some same-week availability and avoid booking routine appointments months ahead without reminders.

## 7. Send practical information with the reminder

Patients sometimes skip appointments because they're unsure about parking, what to bring or costs. Include:

- A map link and parking notes.
- What to bring (ID, medical aid card, referral letter, previous results).
- How payment works.

## 8. Track no-shows by type and time

Look at your data monthly:

- Which days and times have the most no-shows?
- Are new patients more likely to miss appointments than existing ones?
- Do certain appointment types have higher rates?

Adjust your reminders and scheduling based on the patterns.

## 9. Follow up after a missed appointment

A kind follow-up message ("We missed you today — would you like to reschedule?") recovers many patients and shows you care about their health rather than just the fee.

## Keep it compliant

Reminder messages should be administrative and avoid sensitive clinical details. Patients should know how you'll contact them and be able to opt out. See our [POPIA guide for practices](/blog/popia-compliance-medical-practice-website/).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a normal no-show rate for a medical practice?**
It varies widely by speciality and patient population. Measure your own baseline first, then track the impact of reminders and policies over a few months.

**Should I charge for missed appointments?**
Many practices do, provided the policy is clearly communicated in advance and applied fairly. Consider waiving fees for genuine emergencies.

**Are WhatsApp reminders better than SMS?**
WhatsApp messages are often read faster and allow two-way replies, but some patients prefer SMS. Ask patients their preferred channel.

**Can reminders be automated?**
Yes — many booking systems and WhatsApp Business Platform providers send automated reminders. Ask your booking system provider about setting this up.
