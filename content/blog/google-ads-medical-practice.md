Google Ads can fill your appointment book within days — or burn through your budget in a week. The difference comes down to setup, targeting, landing pages and tracking. This guide covers what South African practices need to know before spending a rand.

## When Google Ads makes sense for a practice

Ads work best when patients are actively searching for something you offer, such as:

- A new practice that needs patients before SEO has had time to work.
- High-value services (implants, orthodontics, specialist procedures, physio packages).
- Filling gaps in the diary at a specific location.
- Launching a new service.

If your practice is already fully booked, focus on your website and reviews first.

## Rule #1: Advertise honestly and within the rules

Healthcare advertising is regulated. The HPCSA's ethical rules require advertising to be factual and not misleading, and Google has its own healthcare and medicines policies. In practice this means:

- No guaranteed outcomes ("pain-free in one session").
- No claims of superiority over colleagues ("the best dentist in Durban").
- No exploiting fear or creating unwarranted anxiety.
- Accurate pricing information if you mention prices.
- Prescription medicines can't be promoted in ads.

Read our [HPCSA advertising rules guide](/blog/hpcsa-advertising-rules-marketing-guide/) before writing any ad copy.

## Structure campaigns around services, not your practice

A common mistake is one campaign for "Dr Smith's Practice" with every keyword thrown in. Instead:

- **One campaign per key service or location** (e.g. *Dental implants – Sandton*, *Emergency dentist – Sandton*).
- **Tight ad groups** of closely related keywords.
- **Ads that repeat the patient's search language** and link to a page about that exact service.

## Choosing keywords that bring patients, not browsers

Focus on high-intent searches:

- "dentist near me", "emergency dentist Sandton"
- "physiotherapist Centurion", "sports physio near me"
- "paediatrician Pretoria East"

Add **negative keywords** to block wasted clicks — for example *jobs, salary, course, free, how to become, symptoms* (unless you want informational traffic).

Use **phrase** and **exact** match types at first; broad match can spend your budget quickly on irrelevant searches.

## Location and schedule targeting

- Target a realistic radius around your practice — most patients won't travel across a metro for a GP visit.
- Choose "people **in** your targeted locations", not "interested in".
- Run ads when your reception can answer calls, or make sure online requests are followed up first thing the next morning.

## Landing pages: where most budgets are lost

Sending ad clicks to your homepage wastes money. Each ad group needs a landing page that:

1. Matches the search (an implants ad leads to an implants page).
2. Loads quickly on mobile.
3. Explains the service, what to expect, medical aid and fees policy.
4. Has an obvious call-to-action: tap-to-call, WhatsApp and a short booking request form.
5. Includes trust signals — qualifications, registration, real photos of the practice.

See our [high-converting website breakdown](/blog/high-converting-website-breakdown/) for the anatomy of a page that converts.

## Track calls, forms and WhatsApp — or you're guessing

Before launching, set up conversion tracking for:

- Calls from ads and from the website.
- Form submissions and booking requests.
- WhatsApp button clicks.

Without this, Google's automated bidding can't optimise and you can't tell which keywords bring patients.

## How much should a practice budget?

Budgets depend on competition and location. Specialist and cosmetic dental keywords in Johannesburg or Cape Town cost far more per click than GP searches in smaller towns. Start with a test budget for four to six weeks, measure the cost per enquiry, then scale what works.

## Common Google Ads mistakes in healthcare

- No negative keywords.
- Homepage as the landing page.
- No conversion tracking.
- Ads running when nobody answers the phone.
- "Set and forget" — campaigns need weekly attention at first.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are Google Ads allowed for doctors in South Africa?**
Yes. Practitioners may advertise their services provided the advertising is factual, not misleading, and complies with their council's rules and Google's policies.

**How quickly do Google Ads bring patients?**
Campaigns can generate enquiries within days of launch, but expect four to six weeks of optimisation before performance stabilises.

**Should I run ads or invest in SEO?**
Ideally both: ads deliver immediate enquiries while SEO builds long-term visibility that doesn't cost per click.

**Can I advertise on medical-aid terms?**
You can state factual information such as which medical aids you're contracted with, as long as it's accurate and current.
