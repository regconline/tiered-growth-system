When a patient searches "dentist near me", "GP in Rondebosch" or "paediatrician Pretoria East", Google shows a map with three practices before any normal search results. That block — the **local map pack** — is where most healthcare bookings from Google begin. Here's how to earn a place in it, step by step.

## How Google decides which practices to show

Google uses three broad signals for local results:

- **Relevance** — how well your profile and website match what the patient searched for (services, speciality, conditions treated).
- **Distance** — how close your practice is to the searcher or the location in their search.
- **Prominence** — how well known and trusted your practice is online: reviews, links, mentions in directories and the quality of your website.

You can't move your practice closer to every patient, but you can dramatically improve relevance and prominence.

## Step 1: Complete and optimise your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile (GBP) is the single most important local SEO asset you own.

- **Use your real practice name.** Don't add keywords like "Best Dentist Sandton" to the name — it breaks Google's guidelines and can get your profile suspended.
- **Choose the right primary category** (e.g. *Dentist*, *General practitioner*, *Physiotherapist*) and add relevant secondary categories.
- **List every service** you offer with a short, plain-language description.
- **Add accurate hours**, including public holidays, and keep them updated.
- **Upload real photos** of your building, entrance, reception and treatment rooms (no patients without written consent). Add new photos monthly.
- **Enable messaging and booking links** if your team can respond promptly.
- **Use Google Posts** to share practice news, new services or seasonal health reminders.

> Tip: make sure the address on your profile matches your website and signage exactly — down to "Unit 4" versus "Suite 4".

## Step 2: Earn reviews consistently — and reply to every one

Reviews influence both rankings and patient decisions. The goal isn't a burst of reviews in one week; it's a steady flow.

- Ask every satisfied patient at checkout, and follow up with a short SMS or WhatsApp link.
- Never offer incentives, write reviews on a patient's behalf, or only ask patients you think will be positive.
- Reply to every review within 48 hours. For negative reviews, never confirm that the reviewer is a patient or discuss their care — invite them to contact the practice manager directly.

## Step 3: Build consistent local citations

Citations are listings of your practice name, address and phone number (NAP) on other websites. Consistency matters more than quantity.

Prioritise:

1. Medical directories relevant to your profession and patients.
2. Your professional association's member directory.
3. Medical aid provider lists where applicable.
4. General directories (Yellow Pages-type listings, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect).

Audit existing listings for old addresses or phone numbers — outdated citations confuse both Google and patients.

## Step 4: Build location and service pages on your website

Your website supports your Google profile. Each important service should have its own page, written for patients, that includes:

- What the service is and who it helps.
- What to expect at the appointment.
- Practical information: medical aid, fees policy, parking, accessibility.
- A clear call to action — call, WhatsApp or request a booking.

If you have more than one location, give each its own page with the address, hours, map and directions.

Add **LocalBusiness / MedicalBusiness schema** markup so Google clearly understands your name, address, hours and services.

## Step 5: Make your website fast and mobile-friendly

Most local healthcare searches happen on mobile phones — often on patchy data. A slow website loses patients before the page even loads. Aim for your main pages to load in under 2.5 seconds on mobile. Read more in [how a fast website increases bookings](/blog/fast-website-doubles-sales/).

## Step 6: Earn local links and mentions

Links from reputable local sources boost prominence:

- Community health talks, school or sports-club partnerships.
- Local news features on new services or health awareness days.
- Collaborations with other non-competing health professionals.

## Step 7: Track what matters

Rankings are useful, but the real measure is patients. Track:

- Calls, direction requests and website clicks from your Google Business Profile.
- Website enquiries, WhatsApp clicks and booking requests.
- Which services and suburbs generate the most enquiries.

Review these monthly and double down on what works.

## Common local SEO mistakes

- Keyword-stuffed business names.
- Multiple Google profiles for the same location.
- Inconsistent phone numbers across directories.
- A website without the practice address or a map.
- Ignoring reviews — positive or negative.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long does local SEO take for a medical practice?**
Profile improvements can show results within a few weeks. Competitive searches in large metros usually take three to six months of consistent work.

**Can I rank in suburbs where I don't have a practice?**
It's difficult to appear in the map pack far from your physical address. You can, however, create helpful content for patients in nearby areas and rank in normal search results.

**Do I need a website for local SEO?**
Yes. Google uses your website to confirm relevance and trust, and patients use it to decide whether to book.

**Is local SEO a once-off job?**
No. Reviews, posts, photos and content need regular attention — competitors are improving too.
