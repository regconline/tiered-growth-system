In South Africa, WhatsApp is often the first channel patients reach for — faster than a phone call and easier than email. Used well, WhatsApp Business can reduce phone pressure at reception, cut no-shows and make your practice easier to deal with.

Here's how to set it up properly.

## WhatsApp vs WhatsApp Business vs the API

- **WhatsApp (personal)** — not suitable for a practice.
- **WhatsApp Business app** — free, designed for small businesses, runs on one phone (with linked devices). Ideal for most practices.
- **WhatsApp Business Platform (API)** — for larger practices and groups that need multiple agents, integrations and automated reminders at scale, usually through an approved provider.

Start with the Business app unless you have high volumes.

## Step 1: Use a practice-owned number and device

Use the practice's number (ideally your main landline or a dedicated cellphone number) on a **practice-owned device**. Never run practice WhatsApp on staff members' personal phones — it creates privacy and continuity problems.

## Step 2: Complete your business profile

- Practice name and logo.
- Address, hours and website.
- A short description of your services.
- Email address.

## Step 3: Set up automatic messages

**Greeting message** (sent to new chats):

> "Hello and thank you for contacting [Practice]. For emergencies please call 10177 or go to your nearest emergency unit. For appointments, please send your full name, preferred date and time, and whether you're a new or existing patient."

**Away message** (outside hours):

> "Thank you for your message. Our practice is currently closed. We'll reply when we open at 08:00. For emergencies please call 10177."

## Step 4: Create quick replies

Quick replies save reception hours every week. Create shortcuts for:

- `/hours` — opening hours.
- `/directions` — address, map link and parking.
- `/medicalaid` — medical aid and payment information.
- `/firstvisit` — what to bring to a first appointment.
- `/cancel` — cancellation policy.

## Step 5: Use labels to stay organised

Labels like *New booking*, *Awaiting confirmation*, *Results query* and *Billing* help your team see what needs action.

## Step 6: Add WhatsApp to your website and Google profile

A WhatsApp button on every page of your website lets patients reach you in one tap. Include a pre-filled message so you know where they came from. Add your WhatsApp number to your Google Business Profile too.

## Step 7: Appointment reminders

Reminders are one of the most effective ways to reduce no-shows. With the Business app, reminders can be sent manually or with broadcast lists (patients must have your number saved). For automated reminders at scale, use the WhatsApp Business Platform through your booking system or a provider.

## POPIA and confidentiality rules

WhatsApp is convenient, but health information is sensitive. Agree on a practice policy:

- Patients opt in to WhatsApp communication.
- Keep messages administrative where possible (bookings, directions, payments).
- Don't send results, diagnoses or images of patients unless the patient has consented and you've assessed the risk.
- Lock the device and enable two-step verification on WhatsApp.
- Limit access to authorised staff.
- Never give individual medical advice over WhatsApp without a proper consultation.

Our [POPIA guide](/blog/popia-compliance-medical-practice-website/) covers the wider compliance picture.

## Measure the impact

Track how many bookings come through WhatsApp, average response times and no-show rates before and after. Many practices find WhatsApp becomes their main booking channel within months.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is WhatsApp safe for patient communication?**
WhatsApp messages are end-to-end encrypted, but risks come from lost devices, shared access and oversharing. A clear practice policy and secured devices are essential.

**Can multiple receptionists use one WhatsApp Business number?**
The Business app supports linked devices, so a few team members can work from one number. Larger teams may need the WhatsApp Business Platform.

**Can patients book appointments on WhatsApp?**
Yes. Many practices accept booking requests on WhatsApp and confirm them in their practice management system.

**What should we never send on WhatsApp?**
Avoid sending sensitive clinical information, identity documents or images of patients without consent and a clear policy.
