Patients rarely choose a practice on a whim. Whether they need a GP for a child's fever or a specialist for a long-term condition, most follow a similar path online. Understanding that path helps you show up — and be chosen — at every step.

## Step 1: A need arises

Something prompts the search: symptoms, a referral, a move to a new area, a medical aid change, or dissatisfaction with a current provider. The need shapes what they search for:

- **Urgent:** "doctor open now near me", "emergency dentist".
- **Specific:** "paediatrician Durban North", "sports physio Stellenbosch".
- **Research:** "what causes lower back pain", "do I need a root canal".

**What to do:** make sure your website and Google profile clearly list your services, location and hours — including whether you see urgent cases.

## Step 2: The search results

Patients usually see Google's map pack first, then websites and directories. On a phone, the first screen decides who gets attention.

**What to do:**

- Optimise your [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-doctors-guide/).
- Build service pages that answer specific searches.
- Consider Google Ads for urgent or high-value searches.

## Step 3: The quick comparison

Patients often open two or three practices and compare in under a minute. They look at:

- **Star rating and number of reviews.**
- **Distance and directions.**
- **Opening hours** — are you open when they need you?
- **Photos** — does the practice look clean, modern and welcoming?

**What to do:** keep reviews flowing ([here's how](/blog/get-more-google-reviews-medical-practice/)), upload real photos and keep hours accurate.

## Step 4: The website check

Many patients then visit the website to answer practical and emotional questions:

- Do they accept my medical aid?
- What will it cost?
- Who is the doctor, and are they qualified for my problem?
- Can I book easily?
- Do they seem kind and trustworthy?

**What to do:** answer these on your website. Clear fees information, practitioner bios, a fast mobile site and visible booking options remove doubt. See our [website must-haves](/blog/healthcare-website-must-haves/).

## Step 5: The first contact

Patients call, WhatsApp or submit a form. This moment is often where practices lose them:

- Phones not answered during busy times.
- WhatsApp messages left unread for hours.
- Forms that are never followed up.

**What to do:** set response time standards, use WhatsApp quick replies and make sure someone checks web enquiries several times a day.

## Step 6: The visit and beyond

The experience in the practice determines whether patients return, recommend you and leave a review. Online and offline reputation are connected.

**What to do:** ask for feedback, send helpful follow-ups and run recall reminders.

## What builds trust online

Across every step, the same trust signals matter:

- Accurate, consistent information everywhere.
- Real photos and named practitioners with credentials.
- Recent, genuine reviews with thoughtful replies.
- Educational content that shows expertise without exaggeration.
- Easy, responsive communication.

And just as importantly, what destroys trust: outdated hours, broken links, slow websites, unanswered messages and over-the-top marketing claims.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do patients really read reviews for doctors?**
Yes. Reviews are one of the most visible pieces of information on Google, and many patients check them before booking with someone new.

**What matters more — my website or my Google profile?**
Both work together. The Google profile often creates the first impression; the website answers the questions that lead to a booking.

**How fast should we reply to WhatsApp enquiries?**
As quickly as possible during business hours. Set an away message for after-hours so patients know when to expect a reply.

**How can I find out where my patients come from?**
Ask new patients at registration and use website and call tracking. Our [analytics service](/services/analytics-reporting/) can set this up.
