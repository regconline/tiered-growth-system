Your website is often the first interaction a prospective patient has with your practice. Done well, it builds trust within seconds. Done poorly, it sends them straight to a competitor. These are the ten elements every South African healthcare website needs in 2026.

## 1. It loads fast on a phone

Most patients will find you on a mobile phone, often on mobile data. If your homepage takes longer than about three seconds to appear, many will give up. Compress images, avoid heavy sliders and use modern hosting. Speed also helps your Google rankings — see [how a fast website increases bookings](/blog/fast-website-doubles-sales/).

## 2. Contact options are impossible to miss

Patients should be able to contact you from any page in one tap:

- Tap-to-call phone number in the header.
- A WhatsApp button.
- A short booking request form.
- Your address with a Google Maps link.

## 3. Clear medical aid and fees information

"Do you accept my medical aid?" is one of the most common questions practices receive. Answer it on the website: list the schemes you're contracted with (if any), whether you charge medical aid rates or private rates, and payment options. This reduces calls and builds trust.

## 4. Service pages written for patients

Create a page for each key service explaining, in plain language:

- What it is and who it's for.
- What to expect before, during and after.
- How long it takes and how to prepare.
- When to seek urgent care instead.

Avoid jargon, guarantees and superlatives — they don't build trust and can breach HPCSA advertising rules.

## 5. Real people and credentials

Patients choose people, not logos. Include professional photos and short bios for each practitioner with qualifications, HPCSA (or relevant council) registration and areas of interest. Real photos of your reception and rooms help anxious patients know what to expect.

## 6. Practical visit information

- Hours, including public holidays.
- Parking, wheelchair access and public transport.
- What to bring to a first appointment.
- Cancellation policy.

## 7. Online booking or booking requests

Even a simple "request an appointment" form that your reception confirms can capture patients outside office hours. For busier practices, integrate the booking system you already use.

## 8. POPIA-compliant forms and privacy policy

If your website collects any personal information, you need a privacy policy explaining how it's used and protected. Forms should:

- Collect only what you need.
- Include a consent checkbox.
- Avoid asking for detailed medical information over an unsecured form.



## 9. Accessibility

Use readable font sizes, strong colour contrast, descriptive link text and alt text on images. Accessible sites work better for older patients, people with visual impairments — and for Google.

## 10. Built-in SEO foundations

A healthcare website should be built to rank from day one:

- Unique page titles and meta descriptions.
- Logical headings and internal links.
- Structured data (schema) for your practice.
- Location information consistent with your Google Business Profile.



## Quick self-audit checklist

- [ ] Loads in under three seconds on mobile
- [ ] Phone, WhatsApp and booking visible on every page
- [ ] Medical aid and fees explained
- [ ] A page for each main service
- [ ] Practitioner photos, bios and registration
- [ ] Privacy policy and consent on forms
- [ ] Address, map and hours match Google
- [ ] Every link works

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does a medical practice website cost in South Africa?**
Simple, professional sites typically start from around R6,500, with larger sites featuring booking systems and many service pages costing more. Our [website packages](/services/website-services/) show transparent pricing.

**Can I use patient testimonials on my website?**
Testimonials and endorsements used to promote your services can conflict with HPCSA advertising rules. Focus on factual information, credentials and clear explanations instead.

**How often should I update my website?**
Review key information (hours, fees, practitioners) at least quarterly and add new content regularly to support SEO.

**Should my website include a blog?**
A blog helps you answer common patient questions and rank for more searches — as long as content is accurate and reviewed by a clinician.
