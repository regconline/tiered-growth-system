Dentistry is one of the most competitive healthcare categories online. Patients search for a dentist when they're in pain, when they want a check-up, or when they're considering treatments like implants, aligners or whitening — and they compare practices quickly.

This guide covers the marketing foundations every South African dental practice needs.

## Know your patient journeys

Dental patients usually fall into three groups, each with different needs:

1. **Urgent** — toothache, broken tooth, swelling. They want a nearby practice that can see them today. Speed and phone access matter most.
2. **Routine** — check-ups, cleaning, children's dentistry. They value convenience, medical aid acceptance and a friendly team.
3. **Considered treatments** — implants, orthodontics, cosmetic dentistry. They research for weeks, compare costs and want to trust the dentist's expertise.

Your website and marketing should serve all three.

## 1. A website built for bookings

Your website should make it effortless to book:

- Tap-to-call and WhatsApp on every page.
- A dedicated **emergency dentist** page with same-day instructions.
- Separate pages for key treatments (implants, root canals, children's dentistry, orthodontics, whitening).
- Clear information about medical aids and payment options.
- Photos of your practice and team, plus dentist bios with qualifications.

See [10 must-haves for a healthcare website](/blog/healthcare-website-must-haves/).

## 2. Dominate local search

Most dental searches include a location or "near me". Prioritise:

- A fully optimised [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-doctors-guide/) with *Dentist* as primary category.
- Steady, genuine [Google reviews](/blog/get-more-google-reviews-medical-practice/).
- Consistent listings across directories.
- Location information on your website that matches Google exactly.

## 3. Use Google Ads for high-value and urgent searches

Paid search works particularly well for:

- **Emergency dentist** searches during practice hours.
- **Implants and orthodontics**, where each new patient has high lifetime value.

Send each ad to a dedicated landing page and track calls and WhatsApp clicks. Our [Google Ads starter guide](/blog/google-ads-medical-practice/) explains the setup.

## 4. Educational content that answers real questions

Dental patients ask the same questions repeatedly. Turn them into articles and social posts:

- "How long does a dental implant take?"
- "What to do about a knocked-out tooth"
- "When should my child first see a dentist?"
- "Are clear aligners right for me?"

Content like this builds trust and ranks on Google over time.

## 5. Social media that shows your personality

Patients — especially anxious ones — want to know what your practice feels like. Share:

- Team introductions and behind-the-scenes moments.
- Oral health tips and myth-busting.
- Explanations of procedures and what to expect.
- Practice news and community involvement.

Be cautious with smile transformations: before-and-after images and testimonials can conflict with HPCSA advertising rules. See our [HPCSA advertising guide](/blog/hpcsa-advertising-rules-marketing-guide/).

## 6. Recall and retention

Your existing patients are your most valuable marketing asset. Make sure recall reminders for check-ups and hygiene visits go out consistently by WhatsApp, SMS or email. A good recall system fills the diary without any new marketing spend.

## 7. Measure what matters

Track:

- New patient enquiries per month (calls, WhatsApp, forms).
- Which channels and treatments bring them.
- Cost per new patient for paid campaigns.
- Review growth and rating.

## Frequently asked questions

**What's the best marketing channel for a new dental practice?**
A strong website and Google Business Profile come first. Google Ads can generate early enquiries while SEO and reviews build over time.

**Can dentists show before-and-after photos?**
This is a high-risk area under healthcare advertising rules and requires careful consideration of consent and potential to mislead. Many practices focus on educational content instead.

**How much should a dental practice spend on marketing?**
It depends on your goals, competition and capacity. Start with the foundations, measure cost per new patient, and scale what works.

**How long before dental SEO works?**
Local profile improvements can show results within weeks; competitive treatment keywords often take three to six months.
