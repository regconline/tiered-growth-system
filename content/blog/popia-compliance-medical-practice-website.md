The Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA) applies to every practice that collects personal information — and in healthcare that's every practice. While your clinical records are governed by additional rules, your **website, forms, WhatsApp and marketing** are where many practices unknowingly fall short.

This guide focuses on the marketing side: what your website and patient communication need to be POPIA-aligned.

> This is general guidance, not legal advice. For a formal compliance opinion, speak to a qualified attorney or consult the Information Regulator's guidance.

## Why POPIA matters more in healthcare

POPIA treats **health information** as *special personal information*, which has stricter conditions for processing. Healthcare practices also handle large volumes of personal data every day. The Information Regulator can investigate complaints and impose significant penalties, and a breach can seriously damage patient trust.

## 1. Publish a clear privacy policy

Your website needs a privacy policy that explains, in plain language:

- What personal information you collect (forms, cookies, WhatsApp).
- Why you collect it and how you use it.
- Who you share it with (hosting, email, booking or analytics providers).
- How long you keep it.
- How people can access, correct or delete their information.
- How to contact your Information Officer.

Link to it from your website footer and from every form.

## 2. Collect only what you need

Website forms should collect the minimum: usually name, phone number, email and a short message. **Don't ask for ID numbers, medical aid numbers or detailed medical history on a general contact form.** Collect that information securely at intake instead.

## 3. Get clear consent

- Add a consent checkbox to forms ("I agree that the practice may contact me about my enquiry").
- Don't pre-tick it.
- Separate consent for marketing (newsletters, health tips) from consent to respond to an enquiry.

## 4. Secure your website

- Use HTTPS on every page (the padlock in the browser).
- Keep your website software and plugins updated.
- Use strong passwords and two-factor authentication for website and email admin accounts.
- Make sure form submissions are delivered securely and not stored in insecure places.

## 5. WhatsApp: convenient, but set rules

WhatsApp is how many South African patients prefer to communicate. To use it responsibly:

- Use **WhatsApp Business** on a practice-owned device and number, not personal phones.
- Let patients opt in to reminders and messages.
- Keep messages administrative (appointments, directions, payments) and avoid sending sensitive clinical information unless you have a clear policy and patient consent.
- Limit which staff can access the account.

## 6. Email and SMS marketing

POPIA restricts direct marketing by electronic communication. In general:

- You may market to existing patients about similar services if they were given the chance to opt out when their details were collected.
- New contacts must opt in.
- Every message must include an easy way to unsubscribe.

## 7. Analytics, cookies and ads

Website analytics and advertising pixels collect information about visitors. To stay on the right side:

- Mention analytics and cookies in your privacy policy.
- Configure tools to avoid collecting health information — for example, don't put condition names in URLs of booking confirmation pages that are tracked.
- Avoid retargeting people based on sensitive health pages.

## 8. Appoint and register an Information Officer

Every responsible party must have an Information Officer — by default, the head of the practice. Register them with the Information Regulator and make sure staff know who to escalate privacy questions to.

## 9. Have a breach plan

If personal information is compromised (a hacked email account, a lost phone with patient WhatsApps), POPIA requires notification of the Information Regulator and affected people as soon as reasonably possible. Decide in advance who does what.

## POPIA website checklist

- [ ] Privacy policy published and linked from forms
- [ ] Forms collect only essential information
- [ ] Unticked consent checkbox on forms
- [ ] HTTPS everywhere
- [ ] WhatsApp Business on a practice device
- [ ] Marketing opt-in and unsubscribe options
- [ ] Analytics configured without health data
- [ ] Information Officer registered

## Frequently asked questions

**Does POPIA apply to small practices?**
Yes. POPIA applies regardless of size to anyone processing personal information in South Africa.

**Can patients book through my website contact form?**
Yes, but keep the form simple and avoid collecting detailed medical information through it. Confirm the booking and gather clinical information through secure channels.

**Is Google Analytics allowed under POPIA?**
Analytics can be used if it's disclosed in your privacy policy and configured not to collect special personal information such as health data.

**Do I need consent to send appointment reminders?**
Appointment reminders relate to an existing service relationship, but it's best practice to confirm patients' preferred channel and allow them to opt out.
