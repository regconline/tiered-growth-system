Before booking with a new doctor or dentist, most people check Google reviews. Reviews also influence how prominently your practice appears in Google Maps. Yet many excellent practices have only a handful of reviews — because they never ask.

Here's how to build a steady flow of genuine reviews while staying within healthcare ethics.

## The ground rules

- **Ask everyone, not just happy patients.** Selectively asking only patients you expect to be positive ("review gating") goes against Google's policies.
- **No incentives.** Discounts, gifts or prize draws for reviews are not allowed.
- **Never write reviews yourself** or ask staff, friends or family to post them.
- **Never pressure patients.** A gentle, optional request is enough.

## Step 1: Get your short review link

In your Google Business Profile, find the **"Ask for reviews"** option and copy your short link. You can also generate a QR code for it.

## Step 2: Ask at the right moment

The best time to ask is when the patient is most satisfied — usually at checkout after a good visit. A simple script for reception:

> "Thank you for coming in today. If you have a moment, we'd really appreciate honest feedback on Google — it helps other patients find us. I'll send you the link."

## Step 3: Follow up the same day

Send a short SMS or WhatsApp message with the link within a few hours:

> "Thank you for visiting [Practice name] today. If you'd like to share feedback about your experience, you can leave a review here: [link]. Kind regards, the team."

Keep it optional, friendly and free of clinical details.

## Step 4: Make it visible in the practice

- A small QR code card at reception.
- A line on invoices or appointment confirmations.
- A link in your email signature.

## Step 5: Reply to every review

Replies show prospective patients that you care. Respond within 48 hours.

**Positive reviews:** thank them warmly and briefly. Don't confirm clinical details.

> "Thank you for taking the time to share your feedback. We're glad the team made you feel welcome."

**Negative reviews:** this is where confidentiality matters most. Never confirm that the reviewer is a patient or discuss their care.

> "Thank you for your feedback. We take every concern seriously and would value the opportunity to discuss this privately. Please contact our practice manager on [number]."

Then follow up offline. Many reviewers update their review after a respectful conversation.

## Step 6: Flag reviews that break Google's rules

You can report reviews that are spam, offensive, conflicts of interest (e.g. from a competitor) or unrelated to your practice. Genuine negative reviews can't be removed — but a calm reply often reassures readers more than a perfect score.

## Step 7: Learn from the feedback

Look for patterns: waiting times, phone responsiveness, parking, billing confusion. Fixing these improves patient experience — and future reviews.

## How many reviews do you need?

There's no magic number. Aim to have more recent reviews than your nearest competitors, and to receive new reviews every month. Recency matters to both Google and patients.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it allowed for doctors to ask for reviews?**
Yes. Asking patients for honest feedback is fine. Offering incentives or filtering who you ask is not.

**Can I copy Google reviews onto my website?**
Republishing reviews as promotional testimonials is a grey area under healthcare advertising rules. A safer option is to link to your Google profile so patients can read independent reviews themselves.

**How do I respond to a fake review?**
Flag it to Google with an explanation, then post a brief, neutral reply such as: "We have no record of this interaction. Please contact the practice so we can understand your concern."
