Physiotherapy practices grow through two engines: **direct patients** who search online for help with pain or injury, and **referrals** from doctors, specialists and coaches. The strongest practices build both.

## 1. Be visible for "physio near me"

Most direct patients search locally and urgently — they're in pain and want an appointment this week.

- Optimise your [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-doctors-guide/) with *Physiotherapist* as the primary category.
- List specific services: sports injuries, back and neck pain, post-surgery rehabilitation, dry needling, paediatric physio.
- Upload photos of your treatment rooms and rehab area.
- Build a steady flow of [genuine reviews](/blog/get-more-google-reviews-medical-practice/).

## 2. Create condition and service pages

Patients search for problems, not treatments. Build pages around what they type:

- "Lower back pain treatment in [suburb]"
- "Knee rehab after ACL surgery"
- "Running injury physio"
- "Neck pain from desk work"

Explain how you assess and treat the condition, what a typical treatment plan looks like and when to see a doctor instead. Avoid promising specific outcomes or numbers of sessions.

## 3. Strengthen your referral network

Referrals remain a powerful source of patients:

- Introduce yourself to local GPs, orthopaedic surgeons and sports physicians.
- Send concise, timely progress reports — referrers notice good communication.
- Offer educational sessions for practice staff.
- Make your referral process simple: a referral page on your website with your details and an easy way to book.

## 4. Partner with sports and fitness communities

Clubs, schools, gyms and running groups are natural partners:

- Offer injury-prevention talks or warm-up workshops.
- Provide pitch-side support at events where appropriate.
- Share practical content (stretching routines, return-to-sport advice).

## 5. Use content that genuinely helps

Physiotherapy lends itself to visual content:

- Short videos of simple exercises (with a safety disclaimer).
- Posture tips for remote workers.
- "When to rest vs when to move" explainers.

This content builds trust and is ideal for social media and your blog.

## 6. Focus on retention and completed treatment plans

Many patients stop attending once pain eases, before rehabilitation is complete. Support adherence with:

- Clear explanations of the treatment plan at the start.
- Reminders and progress check-ins via WhatsApp.
- Home exercise programmes sent digitally.

See our guide to [reducing no-shows](/blog/reduce-patient-no-shows/).

## 7. Make medical aid information clear

Patients often worry about physio benefits. Explain on your website which schemes you work with, whether you charge scheme rates, and how referrals affect claims.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do physiotherapists need a doctor's referral in South Africa?**
Patients can generally see a physiotherapist directly, though some medical aid benefits may require a referral. Make this clear on your website.

**Which online channel brings the most physio patients?**
For most practices, Google Maps and local search generate the most direct enquiries, supported by referrals and social media.

**Can physios advertise on Google?**
Yes, provided ads are factual and not misleading. Avoid guaranteed outcomes such as "pain-free in three sessions".
