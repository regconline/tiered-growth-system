When someone searches for a doctor, dentist or physio nearby, Google shows a map with three practices before anything else. Those listings come from **Google Business Profile** (formerly Google My Business). It's free, and for most practices it generates more calls and direction requests than the website itself.

Here's how to set it up properly — and keep it working for you.

## Step 1: Claim or create your profile

1. Search for your practice name on Google Maps. If a listing already exists, choose **"Own this business?"** to claim it.
2. If there's no listing, go to Google Business Profile and add your practice.
3. Use the **exact practice name** on your signage — no extra keywords.

### Practitioner vs practice listings

Google allows individual practitioner profiles as well as a profile for the practice. For a solo practitioner, Google generally recommends a single listing using the practitioner's name and practice. Multi-doctor practices usually have one profile for the practice and may have separate practitioner profiles. Avoid duplicate listings for the same location.

## Step 2: Verify your listing

Google will ask you to verify — usually by video, phone, email or a postcard. Verification can take a few days. Until verified, your edits may not appear.

## Step 3: Choose the right categories

Your **primary category** strongly affects which searches you appear for. Choose the most specific one that fits (e.g. *Dentist*, *Paediatrician*, *Physiotherapist*, *Psychologist*). Add secondary categories for other significant services, but don't add categories you don't genuinely offer.

## Step 4: Complete every field

- **Address** — exactly as on your website and signage.
- **Service area** — only if you visit patients (e.g. home-based care).
- **Hours** — including special hours for public holidays.
- **Phone** — a number that's answered during business hours.
- **Website** — link to your homepage or a location-specific page.
- **Appointment link** — your booking page or booking request form.
- **Description** — a factual 600–750 character summary of who you are, what you offer and where. Avoid superlatives.

## Step 5: Add your services

List each service with a short, plain-language description. This helps Google match you to more searches — "root canal", "sports massage", "child vaccinations" — and helps patients understand what you offer.

## Step 6: Add real, high-quality photos

Profiles with photos receive more engagement. Upload:

- Exterior photos so patients recognise the building.
- Entrance and parking.
- Reception and waiting area.
- Treatment rooms (without patients).
- Team photos.

Add a few new photos every month. Never upload images of patients without written consent.

## Step 7: Turn on messaging (if you can respond)

Messaging lets patients contact you directly from Google. Only enable it if someone can reply quickly — slow responses can hurt the experience.

## Step 8: Publish Google Posts

Posts appear on your profile and show Google that your listing is active. Ideas:

- New practitioners or services.
- Changes to hours over holidays.
- Health awareness days.
- Links to your latest blog articles.

## Step 9: Manage reviews

Reviews are a major ranking and trust factor. Ask every satisfied patient, make it easy with a short link, and reply to every review professionally. See our guide to [getting more Google reviews ethically](/blog/get-more-google-reviews-medical-practice/).

## Step 10: Answer questions

The Q&A section lets anyone ask (and answer) questions. Monitor it, answer accurately, and add your own frequently asked questions — parking, medical aids, wheelchair access.

## Track your results

Google Business Profile shows how many people:

- Called you from the listing.
- Requested directions.
- Visited your website.
- Found you via searches vs. maps.

Review these monthly alongside your website enquiries.

## Common mistakes

- Keyword-stuffed names ("Dr Smith Best GP Sandton").
- Using a P.O. box or virtual office address.
- Duplicate listings.
- Wrong or outdated holiday hours.
- Ignoring negative reviews.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Google Business Profile free?**
Yes, creating and managing a profile is free.

**Why isn't my practice showing on Google Maps?**
Common reasons: the profile isn't verified, the category is wrong, the information is incomplete, or competitors have stronger relevance and reviews. Our [local SEO playbook](/blog/local-seo-medical-practice/) explains how to improve.

**Can I have a profile if I work from a shared medical centre?**
Yes, provided you have a permanent presence there with your own signage and staffed hours. Use your suite number to distinguish your listing.
